## Overview

GingerBros started as a homebrewing experiment and became a **registered Thai company**
making craft, naturally-fermented, non-alcoholic ginger beer. It's the project where I own
the entire chain end to end: the recipe and production, the brand and label design, the
storefront, and the payment plumbing underneath. From the raw ginger to the payment webhook,
it's all mine.

## Full ownership, end to end

Most people who build a store don't make the product; most people who make a product don't
build the store. GingerBros is both, and running the whole thing myself is exactly what makes
it a good engineering project rather than just a business — every layer is a decision I had
to make and live with.

- **The product:** a craft, naturally-fermented, non-alcoholic ginger beer.
- **The brand:** name, label, and identity (see it on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/drinkgingerbros/)).
- **The company:** a properly registered Thai entity.
- **The commerce stack:** the storefront and everything behind the "buy" button.

## Doing payments right

The part I care about most as an engineer is handling money correctly and safely.

**Card data never touches my servers.** The storefront and checkout run on **Stripe**, which
handles cardholder data as a PCI-DSS-compliant processor. That's a deliberate security
decision, not a convenience one: the safest way to handle card data is to architect the
system so that *you never handle it in the first place*. Keeping card data entirely off my
infrastructure keeps my PCI compliance scope to an absolute minimum and removes an entire
category of risk.

**Every payment confirmation is verified before it's trusted.** Payment results come back as
Stripe **webhooks**, and every incoming webhook has its signature verified before my system
acts on it. That means a forged "payment succeeded" call can't trick the store into treating
an order as paid. This matters especially for Thai **PromptPay** payments, which settle
asynchronously — the confirmation arrives separately from the checkout, so trusting the
right signal, and only the right signal, is what keeps orders honest.

## The takeaway

GingerBros is proof I can take something from zero to a real, revenue-capable product —
and that even on a small craft brand, I build the money path the same way I'd build anything
security-sensitive: minimize what you're responsible for, verify what you're told, and never
trust an unauthenticated message.
