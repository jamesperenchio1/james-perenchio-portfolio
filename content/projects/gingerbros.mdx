## Overview

GingerBros started as a homebrewing experiment and turned into a **registered Thai company**
making craft, naturally fermented, non-alcoholic ginger beer. I handle all of it: the recipe
and production, the brand and label design, the online store, and the payment setup behind
it. From the raw ginger to the payment webhook, it's mine.

## Running every part of it

People who build online stores usually don't make the product, and people who make a product
usually don't build the store. I do both here, which means every layer is a decision I made
and have to live with.

- **The product:** a craft, naturally fermented, non-alcoholic ginger beer.
- **The brand:** name, label, and identity (see it on [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/drinkgingerbros/)).
- **The company:** a registered Thai entity.
- **The store:** the storefront and everything behind the "buy" button.

## Handling payments properly

As an engineer, the part I care about most is handling money safely.

**Card data never touches my servers.** Checkout runs on **Stripe**, which handles cardholder
data as a PCI-DSS-compliant processor. I chose that on purpose. The safest way to handle card
data is to design the system so you never have it. Keeping it entirely off my infrastructure
keeps my PCI scope as small as it can be and removes a whole category of risk.

**Payment confirmations are verified before they're trusted.** Payment results arrive as
Stripe **webhooks**, and each one has its signature checked before the store acts on it. A
forged "payment succeeded" request can't mark an order as paid. This matters most for Thai
**PromptPay** payments, which settle asynchronously: the confirmation shows up separately from
the checkout, so the store has to wait for the right signal and ignore anything it can't
verify.
