## Overview

My home lab is where I practice the defensive side of security on infrastructure I fully
own and control. It runs a real stack of self-hosted services for daily use — but the point
isn't the services, it's the access model in front of them. The whole thing is built on one
rule: **nothing is exposed, and nothing is trusted by default.**

It also runs on deliberately modest hardware. This isn't a rack of servers — it's a
low-power **Intel N150**, fully headless, 100% Linux. Part of the exercise is proving that
a rigorous zero-trust setup doesn't require expensive gear; it requires the right architecture.

## The principle: zero trust

Zero trust means no service is reachable simply because you're "on the network." Every
request has to prove who it belongs to first. There's no soft interior — being inside buys
you nothing. That principle drove every decision below.

## The access path

<Diagram />

**No open ports.** Nothing is published by forwarding ports on the router. Every service is
reachable only through a **Cloudflare Tunnel**, which makes an outbound connection from inside
the network. From the public internet there are simply no inbound ports to scan, probe, or
attack — the usual front door doesn't exist.

**Single sign-on in front of everything.** **Authentik** sits as a forward-auth proxy ahead
of *every single service*. Before a request is allowed through to any app, Authentik has to
authenticate it. That centralizes identity into one place: one set of accounts, one policy
surface, one place to allow or revoke a person's access across the whole lab.

**Access is granted deliberately, out of band.** Letting someone in isn't a standing open
door — it's an explicit, per-user approval handled through a messaging-app workflow, so I can
allow or deny access on demand rather than leaving anything permanently open.

## What runs behind it

Behind that access layer is a genuine, useful stack on a **Proxmox** host, all in Docker:

- **Nextcloud** — self-hosted files and collaboration
- **Immich** — a private, self-hosted photo library
- **Grafana** — dashboards and monitoring
- plus other Docker workloads

Everything sits behind the same zero-trust front door, so adding a service never means
opening a new hole — it inherits the tunnel and the SSO automatically.

## Why it matters

This is the counterpart to the offensive practice I do on Hack The Box: somewhere to build
and defend real infrastructure, make deliberate trade-offs, and live with the consequences.
It's the difference between reading about forward-auth, tunnels, and least-privilege access
and actually running them every day.
