## Overview

My home lab is where I practice the defensive side of security on infrastructure I own and
control. It runs services I use every day, plus an isolated range for offensive practice, and
the part I spend the most time on is the access model in front of all of it. The rule is
simple: nothing is exposed to the internet directly, and reaching the practice targets means
going through a gate first, in the browser, every time.

It runs on modest hardware: a headless **Intel N100** mini PC running plain Ubuntu and Docker
— no hypervisor. I wanted to show that a solid zero-trust setup comes down to architecture, not
a rack of servers.

## Zero trust in practice

Being on my network doesn't get a request anywhere. Every request has to clear Cloudflare
Access before it reaches a service, and there's no trusted interior where that check gets
skipped. Each of the decisions below follows from that.

**No open ports.** I don't forward any ports on the router. Services are only reachable
through a **Cloudflare Tunnel**, which opens an outbound connection from inside the network.
From the public internet there are no inbound ports to scan or probe.

**An access gate in front of everything.** **Cloudflare Access** sits in front of every
hostname the tunnel publishes. A request has to authenticate — through Google SSO or a
one-time emailed code — before it's passed through to anything behind it, Guacamole included.

**The attack box stays behind glass.** The parts of the lab built for offensive practice — a
Kali desktop and intentionally vulnerable targets like DVWA and OWASP Juice Shop — have no
direct network path in at all. The only way to reach them is through **Guacamole**, a
browser-based remote desktop gateway, itself sitting behind the same Access check. There's no
SSH or RDP port exposed to the attack box, on this network or any other.

## What runs behind it

Behind the access layer, Docker runs a mix of practice targets and services I actually use:

- **Guacamole** fronting an isolated **Kali** desktop, **DVWA**, and **OWASP Juice Shop** for
  offensive practice against deliberately vulnerable targets
- A self-hosted **Supabase** stack, the same one backing a couple of my other projects
- **ocis** (ownCloud Infinite Scale) with **OnlyOffice** for files and collaborative editing
- **SearXNG** as a private, self-hosted metasearch engine

New services sit behind the same tunnel and access gate by default, so adding one never means
opening a new port.

## How it fits with the rest of my practice

I do offensive practice on Hack The Box, plus the isolated Kali range in this lab. The rest of
the lab is the defensive half: somewhere to build and run real infrastructure, make trade-offs,
and deal with what breaks. Running tunnels, access policies, and least-privilege gateways every
day has taught me more than reading about them did.
