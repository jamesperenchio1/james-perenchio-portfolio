## My focus: client-server communication

A simulator like this is really two programs, a client and a game server, and they have to
stay in step in real time. If they drift apart, the simulation stops feeling real. I took on
the communication layer between them.

We compared options for that layer and chose **WebSockets**. Building it properly meant
getting a solid grip on networking fundamentals:

- **Persistent connections:** keeping a live, low-latency channel open between client and
  server instead of paying the setup cost on every message.
- **Message framing:** defining how state updates are structured and parsed on both ends, so
  nothing arrives ambiguous or malformed.
- **State synchronization:** keeping the client's and server's view of the world consistent
  in real time, which is the hardest part of anything multiplayer or simulated.
