## Overview

PassClass turns a semester of lectures into study material. You give it lecture recordings
and slide decks, and it produces short summaries plus a quiz you can test yourself with. The
answers come from the lecturer's actual material, not from whatever the model picked up on
the open internet.

We built it at a hackathon and it won **Best Use of Redactive AI at UniHack 2024**. I was one
of the team members who built it.

## How it works

The pipeline takes messy, mixed-format course content and turns it into a knowledge base you
can query:

- **Audio to text** with **AWS Transcribe**, so recorded lectures become searchable text.
- **Slides to text** with **AWS Textract**, pulling content out of decks and PDFs.
- **Storage** on **AWS S3** for the raw and processed files.
- **Knowledge base** in **Confluence**, which holds the extracted, structured content.
- **Front end** in **Flask**, a simple interface for summaries and quizzes.

A language model sits on top of that and writes the summaries and quiz questions.

## Privacy by design

The quick way to build this would be to paste all the lecture content into a general-purpose
LLM and ask for summaries. That also hands a university's course material, in bulk, to a
third-party model.

PassClass uses **Redactive's permission-aware RAG** instead. It retrieves only the relevant
chunks of the lecturer's material and sends just those to the model. That has two effects:

- **Answers stay grounded.** Responses are based on what the lecturer actually taught, so
  they match the course.
- **Data stays controlled.** Retrieval is scoped and permission-aware, so course content isn't
  sent wholesale to an outside model.

That design choice is what the judges recognized.

## Stack

Python throughout, AWS (S3, Textract, Transcribe) for ingestion, Confluence as the data
store, OpenAI for generation, Redactive for permission-aware retrieval, and Flask for the
interface.
