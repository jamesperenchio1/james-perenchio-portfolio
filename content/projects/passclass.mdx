## Overview

PassClass turns a semester of lectures into study material. Feed it lecture videos and slide
decks and it produces digestible summaries plus a built-in quizzing function to test yourself
against — all grounded in the lecturer's *actual* material rather than the open internet.

It was built at a hackathon and won **Best Use of Redactive AI at UniHack 2024**. I worked
on it as part of the team.

## How it works

The pipeline takes messy, mixed-media course content and turns it into a clean, queryable
knowledge base:

- **Audio → text** with **AWS Transcribe**, so spoken lectures become searchable text.
- **Slides → text** with **AWS Textract**, pulling the content out of decks and PDFs.
- **Storage** on **AWS S3** for the raw and processed material.
- **Knowledge base** in **Confluence**, which holds the structured, extracted content.
- **Front end** in **Flask** — a simple interface to summarize and quiz.

On top of that sits the language model that writes the summaries and generates quiz questions.

## The part that won the award: privacy by design

The obvious way to build this is to shovel all the lecture content straight into a general
LLM and ask it for summaries. That's also the wrong way — it hands proprietary university
material wholesale to a third-party model.

PassClass does it differently. It uses **Redactive's permission-aware RAG** to retrieve only
the relevant chunks of the lecturer's material and feed *just those* to the model. Two things
fall out of that decision:

- **Answers stay grounded.** Responses are anchored in the lecturer's real content, not the
  model's general knowledge, so they're accurate to the course.
- **Data stays controlled.** Because retrieval is permission-aware and scoped, proprietary
  content isn't dumped indiscriminately into an open third-party LLM.

It's a small but real privacy-by-design decision — and it's exactly what the project was
recognized for.

## Stack

Python throughout, AWS (S3 / Textract / Transcribe) for ingestion, Confluence as the
datastore, OpenAI for generation, Redactive for permission-aware retrieval, and Flask for
the interface.
